# Friday One

Back to the [README](../README.md). Friday One lives on [Main Street](main-street.md), on its heliport in Friday Park across the road from Friday Tower.

Friday One is the office's helicopter: a toon four-seater in Friday's ink with a signal-green stripe and **FRIDAY ONE** down its tail boom. Street admins fly it, anyone can ride along, and everyone in the building sees and hears it go over, from whichever floor they're on and from the roof bar. There's one, for the whole building.

## Getting in and out

Walk up to it while it's down and press **E** within 4 m of either door (both sides have one, just behind the front seats):

- **Fly it**: a street admin (one of the office's admins; on an office with the shared password still on, that's everyone signed in with it) takes the controls, if nobody's there already. One pilot at a time.
- **Ride along**: anyone takes a free passenger's seat, three at most, with or without a pilot aboard yet.
- Someone who isn't a street admin, with nobody at the controls, is told *street admins fly it*, and can still sit in and wait.

The office has the last word on who's aboard: you take your seat once it says yes.

**E** aboard gets you out by your own door once it's down. In the air there's no getting out. Going to another floor mid-flight (from the floor list, say) takes you off it as you go: a pilot gone sends it [flying itself home](#when-the-pilot-goes). Reloading the page mid-flight puts you back on the ground under where it was.

## Flying it

| Key | At the controls |
| --- | --- |
| **Space** | On the ground, spins the rotor up (3 s to full; it lifts at 85%); in the air, climbs (7 m/s) |
| **Shift** | Sinks (5 m/s, and 1.5 m/s within 4 m of the ground) |
| **W / S** (or ↑ ↓) | Forward, up to 28 m/s (about 100 km/h), and back, up to 8 m/s |
| **A / D** (or ← →) | Turn left and right |
| **C** | The chase view or the cockpit view (not V, which is push to talk) |
| Mouse | Looks round: in the cockpit up to 100° either side and 60° up or down; in the chase view it orbits round the helicopter |
| Wheel | How far back the chase view is, 8 to 30 m |
| **E** | Get out, once it's down |

Passengers have the same views, the mouse, the wheel and **E**, and no controls.

With no keys held it slows to a hover, holds its height and levels out; it leans into its speed and banks into its turns. Its engine starts with the first **Space** after you get in and keeps the rotor turning until you get out. It goes no higher than 200 m over the street, and 450 m from the tower is the edge of town, where it's drawn gently back in.

It bumps off anything solid: Friday Tower (its glass bars, the balconies, the back offices on their posts, the marquee, the stairs down its west side, and up on the roof the railing, the bar, the stage, the parasols, the elevator's housing and the mast), the two neighbours behind it, the farm's barn, silo and windmill, the lighthouse, the windsock, Putt Street's kiosk (and the sign on its roof), fence, gate, record board, windmill and arch, whatever stands on a claimed plot (a building site's crane with the whole sweep of its jib), and the mountains, the hills and the tunnel's spur. Trees and street lamps it flies through. A hard bump thunks, shakes the view and says *Mind the building!*. Its tail counts as well as its rotor, and turning beside a wall won't swing the tail into it.

**The views.** The chase view starts 12 m behind it and 4 m up, swings round after it as it turns (and back round behind once you've orbited it with the mouse and flown on), stays at least 1.5 m over the ground and comes in short of anything solid between it and the helicopter. The cockpit view is from your own seat, through the canopy, over the instrument panel; your own head isn't in the way. C switches between them, and the one you used last is the one you get next time. How far the camera sees doesn't change: the haze still closes in past 300 m.

**The chip.** While you're aboard, a chip at the top of the screen shows how high you are over the street, your speed in km/h and your heading (*N 0°*), what it's doing (**HOVER**, **FLYING**, **LANDED**, *Spinning up · 60%*, *Flying itself home*, or why it can't land where it is), and who else is riding along. The hint bar under it shows the keys.

## Setting down

It sets down only slowly (under 3 m/s across the ground and 2 m/s down) and on level ground, with its rotor's disc and its tail clear of everything below. It may always set down on its pad in Friday Park. It never sets down:

- on the lot in front of the garage, where the parachutes come down and the cars go in and out of the garage's mouth;
- on Friday Tower or the lot down its east side;
- on golf's green round the flagstick in Friday Park;
- on the water: the sea, the lake, or within 3 m of either;
- inside Putt Street's fence;
- on a claimed plot (a building site or a shell);
- in the cows' field, or on the park's benches, its map board, the plots' FOR LEASE boards or round the windsock;
- on uneven ground (*Too steep to land*), or past the edge of town;
- on anyone: nobody at street level on any floor within 5 m of its rotor's hub or under its tail (*Someone's underneath*), and no floor's car under it (*There's a car underneath*).

The pilot's page also won't set it down on anything standing on its own floor's street under the skids (a car, a bench, a lamp post) or reaching up into the rotor (a lamp post, a tree trunk).

Over somewhere it can't land, it won't come lower than 3 m: it holds a hover there, and the chip says why. When the office refuses a landing the pilot's page couldn't see coming (someone at street level on another floor, say), it's put back up 3 m with the reason on the chip, and it won't try there again for a few seconds. An accepted landing leaves it parked there when the pilot gets out: land it on the beach and it's still on the beach.

## The downwash

Flying low (10 m or less over the ground there) with its rotor turning fast, it pushes anyone standing on the ground within 7 m out from under it, up to 1.5 m/s, less the further out and the higher it is. Each page moves its own player, the way walking does, so it never puts anyone through a wall. It doesn't push while it's down or spinning down, so passengers can walk straight up to a door, nor anyone up on a balcony or a roof, nor anyone aboard.

## Seeing and hearing it

Everyone on every floor sees it at their own floor's street level, and up on the roof bar it's drawn too, the street a long way down. The pilot's page flies it and tells the office where it is about fifteen times a second; everyone else's page draws it smoothly between those words, carries it on the way it was going for up to 0.3 s when one's late, and only jumps when it's more than 20 m out (after a reload, say). Its rotor spins up and down at its own pace, on the ground too, and blurs into a disc above about 60%.

Who's aboard sits in their seat: the people on your floor as themselves, anyone from another floor (or the roof) as a figure with their name over them. While the pilot isn't on your floor, a tag over the rotor says who's flying it (*🚁 Tyler · Friday One*).

It has a red light on its left, a green one on its right, and a white strobe on its fin and a red beacon on its back that flash while the rotor turns: they glow by themselves and take none of the street's lamps.

You hear it from where it is: the slap of the blades (13 a second at full spin), a quiet turbine whine that rises as the rotor spools up, and wind that grows with speed. It carries across Main Street and fades with distance, it's muffled through the office's glass, and aboard you hear the cabin's low, muffled mix.

Parked or landed, it's solid: you walk round it, not through it.

## When the pilot goes

A pilot who closes the page, loses the connection, or goes to another floor mid-flight leaves it to fly itself home:

1. out from under anything overhead first (the bar of glass that juts out over the tower's west end from about 53 to 81 m up), sliding away from the tower until the way up is clear;
2. straight up to 140 m (clear of the mast) or higher;
3. across at 18 m/s to over its pad;
4. down at 3 m/s, slowly for the last 6 m, and its rotor spins down.

Passengers ride along and are let out by its doors at the pad. If anyone's standing on the pad, it waits at 6 m and asks everyone to step off; after 20 s it sets down instead on the first clear spot it finds 12 to 20 m round the pad.

A pilot who gets out on the ground leaves it parked there, its rotor running down over 6 s. A pilot who's no longer a street admin is moved to a passenger's seat (and it flies itself home, if it's in the air).

## Where it's kept

Where it's parked is kept in the office's data folder's `heli.json` (`{version: 1, pose, pad}`), written each time it's parked or sets down, so a restart finds it where it was. A spot it may no longer be (a plot claimed over it) puts it back on its pad. A `heli.json` that won't read is logged, the helicopter starts at home on its pad, and the file is moved aside (`heli.json.corrupt-<ms>`) before anything is written over it.

## Limits

One helicopter; one pilot, who has to be a street admin; three passengers; getting in and out only while it's down. It doesn't land on buildings, cranes or people, and doesn't land on the roof yet (an H on the roof deck comes later, and so does flying up to a floor's windows to look in). No searchlight, winch, damage, fuel or autorotation.

The flight itself and what it flies in are `shared/heli.ts` and `shared/heli-world.ts`, which the office checks every pose against (`server/heli/`). The props lab shows the model: `/lab/props.html?show=heli&spin=1` (spin 0 to 1; above 0.6 the blades blur).
